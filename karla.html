Karla Caves

Introduction

The **Karla Caves** are ancient **rock-cut Buddhist caves** located in **Maharashtra, India**. Dating back to the **2nd century BCE**, these caves are one of the oldest and most impressive examples of **Indian rock-cut architecture**.

History

The Karla Caves were built during the rule of the **Satavahana dynasty** and served as an important center for Buddhist monks. The caves house inscriptions and sculptures that narrate the **rich cultural heritage** of ancient India.

Architecture

The caves feature a grand **Chaitya Hall** (prayer hall) with **intricately carved pillars, wooden beams, and a beautifully crafted stupa**. The sculptures depict stories from Buddhist mythology, showcasing exceptional artistry.

Visitor Information

Location: Near Lonavala, Maharashtra, India

Timings: 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (Open all days)

Entry Fee: ₹25 for Indians, ₹300 for Foreigners

Best Time to Visit: October to March (Cool & pleasant weather)

How to Reach

The Karla Caves are located near **Lonavala, Maharashtra**. The nearest railway station is **Lonavala**, and the caves are accessible by road. Visitors must climb around **350 steps** to reach the cave entrance.
