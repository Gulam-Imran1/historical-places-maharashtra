Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur

Introduction

Deekshabhoomi is a sacred monument in Nagpur, Maharashtra, where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, along with his followers, converted to Buddhism on 14th October 1956. It is an important pilgrimage site for Buddhists and a symbol of social justice.

History

Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar embraced Buddhism at Deekshabhoomi, renouncing the caste-based social hierarchy. This event marked a significant moment in Indian history and inspired millions to follow Buddhism.

Architecture

The stupa at Deekshabhoomi is one of the largest hollow stupas in the world. Its design represents Buddhist architectural styles with intricate carvings and a peaceful ambiance.

Significance

Deekshabhoomi is a major site for Buddhist pilgrimages and celebrations, especially on 14th October every year, when millions gather to commemorate the conversion ceremony.

Visitor Information

Location: Nagpur, Maharashtra, India

Timings: 6:00 AM to 8:00 PM

Entry Fee: Free

Best Time to Visit: October to February

How to Reach

Deekshabhoomi is well connected by road and is located about 6 km from Nagpur Railway Station and 8 km from Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar International Airport.
