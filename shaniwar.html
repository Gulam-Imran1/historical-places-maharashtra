Shaniwar Wada

Introduction

Shaniwar Wada is a historic fortification in Pune, Maharashtra, built in 1732. It served as the seat of the Peshwas of the Maratha Empire until the British invasion.

History

Constructed by Bajirao I, Shaniwar Wada was a symbol of the Maratha Empire's strength. It was largely destroyed by a fire in 1828, but its ruins still stand as a reminder of its glorious past.

Architecture

The fort originally had seven stories, with intricately carved wooden gates, grand halls, and elaborate fountains. The main entrance, Delhi Darwaza, remains a notable feature.

Legends & Ghost Stories

Shaniwar Wada is famous for its haunted reputation. Locals claim to hear the cries of a young prince, Narayanrao, who was assassinated within the fort. Many visitors and locals believe in the fort's paranormal activities, making it one of India's most haunted places.

Significance

Shaniwar Wada is a major tourist attraction and holds cultural and historical significance. It is also famous for its association with Marathi folklore and ghost stories.

Visitor Information

Location: Pune, Maharashtra, India

Timings: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM

Entry Fee: ₹25 for Indians, ₹300 for Foreigners

Best Time to Visit: October to March

How to Reach

Shaniwar Wada is easily accessible by road and is located in the heart of Pune. Pune Railway Station is about 3 km away, and Pune Airport is around 12 km from the site.
